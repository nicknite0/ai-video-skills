# AUDIO SKILL

## PURPOSE

Direct the audio of the generated scene.

The user's prompt defines what happens and what is spoken. This skill defines how the scene should sound.

Prioritize believable human vocals, clear dialogue performance, synchronized speech, natural environmental sound, and consistent sound perspective.

Do not change the story, dialogue text, characters, actions, or visual direction unless required to support the requested audio.

## 🟡 NEW — TESTING: VOCAL IDENTITY

When a character speaks, establish a believable vocal identity from information already present in the prompt.

Use the character's apparent age, role, physical presence, emotional state, and situation when choosing the voice.

Keep the same speaker's vocal identity consistent throughout the scene.

When multiple characters speak, keep their voices naturally distinguishable.

Do not invent unsupported accents, dialects, speech disorders, or exaggerated vocal traits.

## 🟡 NEW — TESTING: NATURAL SPEECH

Speech should sound like a person naturally speaking in the physical scene.

Use natural conversational rhythm, cadence, pauses, hesitation, breathing, emphasis, and phrasing when appropriate.

Avoid announcer, narrator, synthetic, overly theatrical, overly polished, sing-song, or text-to-speech-style delivery unless explicitly requested.

Do not rush spoken words merely to fit the clip.

Preserve the exact spoken words requested by the user. Do not add, remove, paraphrase, repeat, or reorder dialogue.

## 🟡 NEW — TESTING: PRONUNCIATION AND SEQUENCES

When the prompt contains counting, letters, names, short phrases, or ordered spoken sequences, pronounce each requested item clearly and in the correct order.

Maintain natural spacing between items rather than blending, skipping, duplicating, or rushing them.

Do not add explanatory speech before or after the requested sequence.

## 🟡 NEW — TESTING: VOCAL PERFORMANCE

Match vocal delivery to the character's immediate behavior and emotional state.

Use appropriate speaking pace, intensity, projection, restraint, pauses, and breath.

A quiet nearby conversation should feel intimate and physically close. A call across a large space should naturally project farther.

Keep emotional performance believable and restrained unless the prompt explicitly calls for heightened performance.

## 🟡 NEW — TESTING: SPEECH SYNCHRONIZATION

When a visible character is speaking, the audible speech should correspond to that character's speaking performance.

Coordinate the start and end of speech with visible mouth movement and the character's physical performance.

Do not continue visible speaking after the requested line has ended.

Do not assign one character's dialogue to another character.

## 🟡 NEW — TESTING: SOUND PERSPECTIVE

Sound should belong to the physical environment shown in the scene.

Match voices and sound effects to apparent distance, direction, room size, openness, and surrounding surfaces.

Nearby sounds should feel nearby. Distant sounds should feel distant.

Use natural room tone and environmental acoustics with restraint.

Avoid excessive echo, reverb, filtering, or artificial effects unless the environment or prompt requires them.

## 🟡 NEW — TESTING: ENVIRONMENT AND FOLEY

Include natural environmental sound and believable Foley that directly supports visible actions.

Examples include footsteps, clothing movement, object handling, doors, tools, machinery, wind, rain, room tone, and surface contact when appropriate.

Keep supporting sounds subordinate to important dialogue.

Do not invent distracting sound events unrelated to the scene.

## AUDIO RESTRAINT

Never add narration, voice-over, internal monologue, spoken exposition, dialogue, or vocalizations that were not requested or clearly required by the user's prompt.

Do not add background music unless explicitly requested.

Do not add cinematic trailer voices, commentary, labels, announcements, or explanatory speech.

The user's explicit audio, dialogue, vocal, music, and sound instructions always take priority.
