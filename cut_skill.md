# CUT DIRECTION SKILL

## PURPOSE

Direct cuts and shot transitions so edits feel intentional, readable, and continuous.

The user's prompt defines WHAT happens.
This skill directs HOW the scene moves from one shot to another.

Do not add cuts when a continuous shot is clearly requested.

## 🟡 NEW — TESTING: CUT INTENT

Every cut should have a clear reason.

A cut may:
- reveal new information
- emphasize an important detail
- show a reaction
- change viewpoint
- improve action clarity
- shift attention
- change scale from environment to subject or detail
- advance the scene to the next meaningful beat

Avoid random coverage, unnecessary angle changes, and cuts that do not improve the scene.

## 🟡 NEW — TESTING: CUT TIMING

Cut at a moment that supports the action, information, or emotion.

When useful:
- CUT ON ACTION — carry readable movement across the edit
- CUT ON REACTION — move to the character whose response matters
- CUT ON REVEAL — change shot when new information becomes important
- CUT ON ATTENTION — follow a character's gaze or attention to its target
- HOLD — do not cut too early when a strong composition or performance needs time

Do not interrupt important actions or reactions without a reason.

## 🟡 NEW — TESTING: CUT CONTINUITY

Preserve the scene state across a cut unless the prompt explicitly changes it.

Maintain important:
- CHARACTER IDENTITY — same person, clothing, appearance, and condition
- PROP STATE — held or important objects remain present and in the correct state
- ACTION STATE — movement continues from a believable point
- BODY POSITION — pose and orientation remain compatible with the preceding shot
- SPATIAL RELATIONSHIP — characters and objects remain in understandable locations
- SCREEN DIRECTION — movement and facing remain readable across the edit
- ENVIRONMENT — location and important surroundings remain consistent
- STORY STATE — completed events remain completed and established information remains true

Do not make an important object disappear, appear, duplicate, transform, or change hands merely because the camera cuts.

## 🟡 NEW — TESTING: VISUAL CONNECTION

The shot after a cut should connect clearly to the shot before it.

Useful connections include:
- matching action
- matching eyeline
- matching subject attention
- matching spatial geography
- matching story information
- deliberate visual or compositional relationship

A large change in framing or camera position is allowed when continuity remains understandable.

Do not confuse visual similarity with continuity. A strong cinematic cut may look very different while still preserving the scene state.

## 🟡 NEW — TESTING: SHOT / REVERSE SHOT

For conversations or opposing subjects, preserve understandable eyelines, facing, and relative positions.

Each subject should appear to look toward the correct person or point of interest.

Avoid accidental side swaps or contradictory eyelines unless a deliberate spatial change has been established.

## 🟡 NEW — TESTING: TRANSITION CHOICE

Use the simplest transition that serves the scene.

HARD CUT — normal direct edit between shots.

FADE — use only when a deliberate opening, ending, or major temporal separation calls for it.

DISSOLVE — use only when a softer passage of time, memory, or intentional visual overlap is appropriate.

MATCH CUT — connect shots through deliberate action, shape, composition, or story relationship.

HIDDEN CUT — conceal an edit through motion, occlusion, darkness, or another natural visual bridge when useful.

WHIP TRANSITION — use rapid camera motion only when the scene's energy supports it.

SMASH CUT — use an intentionally abrupt contrast only when the story beat calls for it.

Do not add decorative transitions without a narrative or visual reason.

## 🟡 NEW — TESTING: AUDIO ACROSS CUTS

When audio is present, preserve believable sound continuity across edits.

Dialogue, ambience, and relevant scene sounds should not restart, disappear, or change unnaturally merely because the camera cuts.

J-cuts or L-cuts may be used when they create a natural connection between shots or scenes.

Do not add narration, dialogue, music, or sound effects that the prompt does not call for.

## RESTRAINT

Do not force frequent cuts.
Do not turn every action into a new shot.
Do not use editing to compensate for unclear staging.
Do not rewrite the user's story.
Do not introduce new characters, props, locations, or events solely to create an edit.

Explicit user instructions about cuts, transitions, shot order, continuity, or continuous takes have priority.
