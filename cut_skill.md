# CUT / TRANSITION DIRECTION SKILL

## PURPOSE

Choose and execute cinematic transitions between shots when a transition naturally benefits the scene.

The user's prompt defines WHAT happens.
This skill focuses only on HOW one shot transitions into the next.

Do not force a transition when the scene works better as a continuous shot.

## 🟡 NEW — TESTING: TRANSITION CHOICE

Use transitions only when the scene naturally needs to move from one shot to another.

Choose one transition behavior that fits the moment. Do not cycle through transition types or add extra edits just because multiple choices are available.

- HARD CUT — End the current shot and immediately begin a new shot from a different camera view. Do not blend, fade, morph, or move the camera between the two shots.
- MATCH CUT — End the first shot on a clear action, movement, shape, subject, or composition. Begin the next shot with a visually matching action, movement, shape, subject, or composition so the edit connects clearly.
- HIDDEN CUT — End one shot while the image is naturally obscured by motion, darkness, a foreground object, or another visual obstruction. Begin the next shot while that obstruction still hides the edit, then reveal the new view.
- WHIP TRANSITION — End the first shot with a fast directional camera move that creates strong motion blur. Begin the next shot with matching directional motion, then settle into the new view.
- SMASH CUT — End one shot abruptly and immediately begin a strongly contrasting new shot with no blend or fade between them.
- DISSOLVE — Briefly overlap the end of the first shot with the beginning of the next so one image gradually replaces the other.
- FADE — Gradually darken or lighten the current image until the shot disappears, then begin the next shot after the separation.

Prefer a normal HARD CUT when no special transition behavior is clearly motivated.

Fast action does not automatically require more cuts.
Do not create a regular cutting rhythm or cut every few seconds.
Let the action, reaction, reveal, time change, or location change create the reason for the transition.

## RESTRAINT

Do not force frequent cuts.
Do not add decorative transitions.
Do not turn every action into a new shot.
Do not invent dialogue, narration, characters, props, locations, or events to create a transition.

Explicit user instructions about transitions, cuts, continuous takes, time changes, or location changes have priority.
