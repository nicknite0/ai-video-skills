# CUT / TRANSITION DIRECTION SKILL

## PURPOSE

Choose and execute cinematic transitions between shots when a transition naturally benefits the scene.

The user's prompt defines WHAT happens.
This skill focuses only on HOW one shot transitions into the next.

Do not force a transition when the scene works better as a continuous shot.

## 🟡 NEW — TESTING: TRANSITION CHOICE

Choose the transition that best serves the action, emotion, visual relationship, change in time, or change in location.

Available choices:

- HARD CUT — direct change to the next shot when a clean immediate edit best serves the scene.
- MATCH CUT — connect shots through related action, movement, shape, composition, subject, or story meaning.
- HIDDEN CUT — conceal the transition through natural motion, foreground occlusion, darkness, a passing object, or another visual bridge.
- WHIP TRANSITION — use rapid camera motion to bridge shots when the scene's energy and movement naturally support it.
- SMASH CUT — use a deliberately abrupt contrast when a sudden dramatic change is appropriate.
- DISSOLVE — use a softer overlap when passage of time, memory, dreamlike connection, or gentle transition is appropriate.
- FADE — use when a clear opening, ending, major passage, or separation is appropriate.

Choose rather than cycle through transition types.
Do not use a transition merely because it is available.
Prefer the simplest transition that communicates the scene clearly.
Fast action does not automatically require rapid cutting or flashy transitions.
Allow action to determine the edit naturally.

## RESTRAINT

Do not force frequent cuts.
Do not add decorative transitions.
Do not turn every action into a new shot.
Do not invent dialogue, narration, characters, props, locations, or events to create a transition.

Explicit user instructions about transitions, cuts, continuous takes, time changes, or location changes have priority.
